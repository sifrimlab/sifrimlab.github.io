01

Multi-omic and spatial data integration

Modern biology generates data across many molecular layers: genome, transcriptome, proteome, and more. We build computational methods that jointly analyse these heterogeneous datasets to reveal biological patterns invisible in any single modality.

MIMA is a modular, unsupervised framework for multi-omics integration and batch correction. Applied to spatial and single-cell datasets, it removes batch effects while preserving biologically relevant information, learns representations that are predictive of expert pathologist annotations, and enables cross-modal translation. This line of work points towards molecular digital pathology: combining high-dimensional molecular data with histopathological imaging for patient stratification.

Integration starts before the model. FOCUS takes raw spatial transcriptomics, mass spectrometry imaging, Raman spectroscopy imaging and microscopy data through preprocessing, alignment and resolution matching onto a shared observation grid. Multiverse makes the evaluation of integration methods reproducible by building benchmarks from containerised components.

Methods & resources

    MIMA

    A modular, unsupervised deep learning framework for multi-omics integration and batch correction. Modality-specific encoders feed shared, modality-specific and batch latent spaces, fused by a product of experts, which enables cross-modal translation.

02

Explainable AI for multi-omic representation learning

Building accurate models is not enough; we need to understand what they learn. We are developing explainability methods tailored to multi-omic settings, enabling researchers to trace model predictions back to interpretable biological features such as gene programmes, regulatory elements, and cell states. This work bridges the gap between black-box deep learning and actionable biological insight.

Standard attribution methods treat input features as independent. When features are causally related, they tend to credit a downstream correlate, for example a target gene, rather than the upstream driver, such as the transcription factor that regulates it. Causal Integrated Gradients embeds a known causal graph in the integration path, decomposing each feature’s attribution into a direct effect and effects mediated through its causal descendants, at the computational cost of standard Integrated Gradients. On simulated single-cell expression data with a known gene regulatory network, it most accurately recovers the transcription factors that drive the simulation.

We also use feature attribution methods to investigate the inner workings of multi-omic integration models. With MIMA and the prostate cancer dataset from that work as a case study, the aim is to uncover the biological signals these models learn and the molecular features, across modalities, that may contribute to prostate cancer biology.

Methods & resources

  • Method

    Causal Integrated Gradients

    Gradient-based feature attribution that follows the causal structure of the inputs. Each feature's attribution is split into a direct effect and effects mediated through its causal descendants, while keeping the completeness property of Integrated Gradients.

    PaperCodeDocumentation

03

Subcellular RNA organisation and spatial transcriptomics

Where an RNA molecule sits inside a cell is not random: subcellular localisation is tightly regulated and functionally important. Imaging-based spatial transcriptomics now measures hundreds to thousands of transcript species at subcellular resolution, making a systematic, genome-wide study of localisation possible.

SubCellSpace represents the localisation of each gene in each cell as an image and embeds it, with a self-supervised autoencoder, into a low-dimensional latent space that encodes subcellular patterning. Around this embedding we built a probabilistic framework for pattern detection that controls false positives, together with a bounded metric that can be compared across datasets. Alongside the method we released a dedicated ground-truth benchmark: a Xenium dataset of HEK293T cells that combines APEX-seq-validated localised transcripts with expression-matched controls. On a MERFISH dataset of the mouse small intestine, SubCellSpace recovers apical–basal mRNA polarisation in enterocytes without predefined annotations.

Targeted spatial transcriptomics also requires choosing a gene panel before the experiment, which cannot be corrected afterwards. We study how this choice is made in practice. Across 18 computational panel-selection tools, only 6.5% of 540 citing records reported using the tool for panel design, and in an independent corpus 34 of 479 studies (7.1%) involved a computational tool at all.

04

Genetic perturbation of the cellular lipidome

Lipids are extraordinarily diverse and central to cellular function, yet we still have a limited understanding of the genetic mechanisms that shape the cellular lipidome. A major challenge has been the lack of scalable approaches that can connect defined genetic perturbations to changes in lipid composition.

LipoGrid arrays CRISPR/Cas9-perturbed cells on a micropatterned grid and sequentially captures their lipidome, by spatial mass spectrometry, and their gRNA identity, complemented by single-cell RNA sequencing of matched cell populations. In a first screen we quantified 158 lipid species across 143 target genes. Most knockouts measurably altered lipid composition, often in specific lipid classes. The screen recapitulates known enzyme–substrate specificities and disease-associated loss-of-function phenotypes, and the joint transcriptomic and lipidomic profiles reveal compensatory feedback mechanisms that buffer the lipidome against genetic perturbation.

LipoGrid is a collaboration between five KU Leuven teams within LISCO: Thierry Voet, Jeroen Lammertyn, Johannes Swinnen, Bernard Thienpont and Alejandro Sifrim.

Methods & resources

  • Screening platform & data browser

    LipoGrid

    A high-throughput multi-omics perturbation screen: pooled CRISPR knockouts on a micropatterned grid, single-cell lipidomes by MALDI mass spectrometry imaging with gRNA detection, and parallel scRNA-seq, explorable in an interactive data browser.

05

Genetic architecture of human disease

Genome sequencing produces millions of variants per individual, the vast majority of which are benign. Identifying the small subset responsible for disease is a critical bottleneck in clinical genetics. As part of the DeepHeart project, we are developing deep learning models that integrate variant features, gene expression, and patient phenotypes to prioritise candidate variants in congenital heart disease, improving diagnostic yield for patients. In the related DeepHeartNet project, long-read whole-genome sequencing is used to identify gene–phenotype associations and capture complex genetic variation in non-syndromic congenital heart disease.

A second direction asks how variation acts through the regulatory genome: we develop deep neural network architectures that predict chromatin accessibility from long-read sequencing data, to study the impact of genetic variation in cancers such as sarcomas.

This work builds on the group’s long-standing contributions to variant prioritisation by genomic data fusion (Sifrim et al., Nature Methods 2013) and to the genetic architecture of congenital heart disease (Sifrim et al., Nature Genetics 2016).

06

Single-cell and spatial biology with LISCO partners

As members of the Leuven Single-Cell Institute (LISCO), we work closely with experimental groups to help them get the most from their single-cell and spatial data. A key focus is end-to-end analysis pipelines for marker gene identification, from raw count matrices to biologically validated cell-type signatures. These collaborations drive new methodological questions and ensure our tools are tested on real and diverse datasets.

  • Oligodendroglia in ALS. A single-nucleus atlas of more than one million cells from human motor cortex and spinal cord shows that “disease-associated oligodendrocytes” are an activation state rather than a distinct subpopulation, and identifies LRP2 as an oligodendrocyte marker that is elevated in ALS cerebrospinal fluid.
  • Neuron loss in Alzheimer’s disease. With the Lab of Neuropathology, a deep learning model trained on more than 100,000 annotated objects detects and classifies neurons in histological images with 95% accuracy.
  • Skin expansion. Under mechanical stretch, fibroblasts re-enter the cell cycle, reduce collagen production and adopt an embryonic-like state that enables epidermal stem cells to self-renew.
  • Nutrient sensing in the gut. Single-cell analysis helped decode how different nutrients activate distinct ensembles of enteric neurons.
